# Sol — anisotropic solvable geometry

**Model and metric.** Use ℝ³ with
ds²=e^(−2z)dx²+e^(2z)dy²+dz².
The central paper §11 writes the left action of (x,y,z) by scaling horizontal coordinates oppositely as height changes. Equivalent group notation is (x,y,z)·(x′,y′,z′)=(x+e^z x′,y+e^(−z)y′,z+z′).

**Observer consequence.** Sol is strongly anisotropic. Rising changes the metric cost of the x and y directions oppositely. The central project reports that some geodesics turn back and that horizontal planes acquire surprising apparent shapes; neither effect can be predicted from a fixed Euclidean camera frame.

**Propagation.** The central paper §11.2 uses a Lie-group method for geodesic flow and parallel transport, with regimes depending on initial velocity. It contains special cases and a generic case rather than one casual Euclidean update rule.

**Quotients.** Compact Sol manifolds arise from ℤ²⋊_Mℤ with hyperbolic M∈SL(2,ℤ), as recorded in the central paper table. A domain wrap must apply the full affine action and its tangent derivative.

**Numerical boundary.** Exponential coordinate scaling creates conditioning issues. Validate metric speed, group action, and transport orthogonality separately from picture plausibility.
