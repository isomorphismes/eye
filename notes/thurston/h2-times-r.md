# H²×ℝ — hyperbolic product geometry

**Model and metric.** Points are (q,z), q∈H², z∈ℝ, with g=g_{H²}+dz². A geodesic splits into a constant-speed H² geodesic and a linear vertical coordinate; the squared speeds add.

**Observer consequence.** A ray combines horizontal exponential spreading with an independent vertical drift. Unlike H³, sectional curvature depends on the 2-plane: horizontal planes have negative curvature while planes containing the ℝ direction have zero curvature. This anisotropy is visible even though the propagation factorizes.

**Connection.** The product connection carries a horizontal tangent vector by H² parallel transport and keeps the vertical component constant. An observer frame that tilts between horizontal and vertical requires both parts; treating vertical as a screen-space axis after arbitrary motion loses the metric relation.

**Quotients.** A Fuchsian group can quotient the H² factor, while an optional vertical translation can compactify or twist the other factor. Scene images then arise from the joint action.

**Numerical boundary.** H² closed forms plus a linear coordinate simplify propagation. Distance to composite objects and quotient image enumeration remain independent algorithms.
