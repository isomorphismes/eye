# H³ — negative constant curvature

**Model and metric.** The hyperboloid model is {x∈ℝ³,¹ : ⟨x,x⟩_L=−1, x₀>0}, with the induced positive metric from the Lorentz form. For p on the hyperboloid and unit tangent v, γ(t)=cosh(t)p+sinh(t)v.

**Observer consequence.** Nearby geodesics diverge exponentially in generic transverse directions. The field of view can contain many distant quotient images; ordinary Euclidean-looking chart distances are not intrinsic distances. The central paper treats H³ among isotropic geometries and supports both model-specific and geometry-independent operations.

**Invariants.** Lorentz norm of p stays −1, tangency ⟨p,v⟩_L stays zero, and g(γ̇,γ̇)=1. Constant sectional curvature is −1. The exponential map has no positive-curvature antipodal collapse, but quotient image multiplicity remains a global issue.

**Quotients.** Hyperbolic manifolds arise from discrete isometry groups. A renderer may work in a Dirichlet domain with generator crossings or search lifted images. Both require an explicit group representation and derivative action on frames.

**Numerical boundary.** Hyperbolic functions grow quickly. Renormalize against the Lorentz constraint and distinguish model-coordinate overflow from intrinsic large distance.
