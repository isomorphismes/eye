# Nil — Heisenberg geometry

**Model and metric.** One common convention uses ℝ³ with group law
(x,y,z)·(x′,y′,z′)=(x+x′,y+y′,z+z′+½(xy′−yx′)).
With coframe dx, dy, θ=dz+½(y dx−x dy), take ds²=dx²+dy²+θ². The sign of θ changes with the group-law convention; a source must carry both together.

**Observer consequence.** Nil is homogeneous but not isotropic. Horizontal and fiber directions have different geometric behavior. The central paper’s §§9.1–9.9 treats a Heisenberg and a rotation-invariant model, exact geodesic flow and parallel transport, distance underestimators, lighting, and discrete subgroups. It reports striking multiple-geodesic phenomena near the central axis.

**Connection and propagation.** Left translation moves a reference frame from the identity to a point, but it is not generally parallel transport. The paper uses a Lie-group method to separate a body velocity curve from an SO(3) transport matrix. Eye should preserve that conceptual split.

**Quotients.** Compact Nil manifolds use lattices; fundamental-domain crossing is a noncommutative group operation. The order of generators matters.

**Numerical boundary.** Coordinate Euclidean radius is not Nil distance. Exact formulas or validated underestimators must replace a naïve Euclidean SDF step.
