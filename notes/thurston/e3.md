# E³ — Euclidean reference geometry

**Model and metric.** E³ is ℝ³ with ds²=dx²+dy²+dz². Cartesian coordinate vectors give a global orthonormal parallel frame, so Γᵏᵢⱼ=0 in that chart and γ(t)=p+t v.

**Observer consequence.** This is the one geometry where a direction can be represented by the same coordinate triple everywhere without further transport data. That is not the definition of a ray; it is a special trivialization of the tangent bundle. A camera frame may be stored as an orthogonal matrix and a world point as a vector, but Eye should regard this as an optimization of the general observer state.

**Invariants.** Unit speed is |v|=1. Parallel transport keeps Cartesian components constant. Curvature is zero. A Euclidean signed-distance field is 1-Lipschitz, making its usual sphere-tracing step rule available when the scene SDF is exact or conservatively bounded.

**Quotients.** A three-torus ℝ³/Λ identifies faces by translations. The position may be reduced modulo a lattice basis, but the tangent frame is unchanged because the derivative of a translation is identity. Rotational or reflection quotient generators do act nontrivially on the frame.

**Numerical boundary.** E³ is the reference test: any generic Eye interface should reproduce affine rays, constant velocity, and unchanged parallel frames before its curved implementation is trusted.
